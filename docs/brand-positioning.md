## One-liner

Dance is an event planning workspace where teams and event agents collaborate on one live picture — scope, timeline, budget, and status — instead of scattered docs and threads.

---

## Audience

**Primary:** Marketing, field, and ops teams who run in-person events.

**Event types we speak to:** Conferences, executive dinners, tradeshows, community events, happy hours, offsites — anywhere alignment and follow-through matter.

**Buyer mindset:** Already stretched thin on logistics. Wants fewer coordination fires, more room for creative strategy, and confidence the event will run as intended.

---

## Positioning

| Layer | Message |
|-------|---------|
| **Category** | Event planning workspace (not generic project management) |
| **Promise** | The smartest way to plan events |
| **Proof** | One live plan — define scope, generate timelines, track budget, collaborate with teammates and agents |
| **Differentiator** | Agents live in the event plan and take real action; they don't just store tasks or give generic advice |

### What we are

- A singular workspace for event teams
- Built for events: templates, live plans, budget tracking, agent coordination
- Alignment as insurance — one source of truth from kickoff through show day

### What we are not

- A Notion or Asana replacement for general work management
- An enterprise ROI / procurement story (not yet — lead with planning quality of life)
- A chatbot that gives advice without context or action

---

## Messaging pillars

### 1. Design events, not spreadsheets

Event planning today lives in Zooms, emails, Google docs, and Notions that go stale. Dance replaces that fragmentation with a visual, live plan.

### 2. One place to execute, together

Outline event properties and scope once. Generate phased timelines with tasks, assignees, and priorities. Keep statuses visible so the team moves forward without double work.

### 3. Agents as teammates

Agents handle repetitive coordination — drafting follow-ups, updating statuses, surfacing blockers, pulling context from connected tools. Humans stay in control; agents do the busywork.

### 4. Room for creativity

Offload tracking and chores so organizers can strategize at a higher level and build experiences that actually move the needle.

## Product vocabulary

Use these terms consistently across marketing, blog, and glossary content.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Event plan** | The live workspace for one event — scope, timeline, budget, owners, status |
| **Event agents** | AI teammates that take action inside the plan (not generic chat) |
| **Define** | Configure event properties and scope visually |
| **Plan** | Generate and maintain phased timelines and tasks |
| **Collaborate** | Work alongside teammates and agents with clear statuses |
| **Templates** | Reusable starting points — never start from step 1 |
| **Budget tracking** | Live view of spend against approved budget |
| **Alignment** | Shared understanding of scope, owners, and progress |

---

## Tone of voice

- **Clear and direct** — say what Dance does in plain language
- **Practitioner-first** — write for people who've lived the spreadsheet-and-Slack chaos
- **Confident, not hypey** — show concrete outcomes (timeline drafted, budget flagged, owner identified)
- **Human + capable** — agents help; they don't replace judgment

**Avoid:** buzzword soup, "revolutionary," vague AI claims, enterprise procurement language.

---

## Key proof points (differentiator examples)

When contrasting Dance vs spreadsheets and docs, use specific, event-native scenarios:

- Budget standing for a conference → live line items, overages flagged, open POs surfaced
- Task ownership for an exec dinner → assignee, shortlist status, vendor thread context
- Brief → timeline → phased plan with owners and default priorities, ready to review
